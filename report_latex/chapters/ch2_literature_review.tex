\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Overview of the Domain}
Financial market surveillance is a multidisciplinary domain sitting at the intersection of quantitative finance, distributed computing, information theory, and financial economics. Capital markets function on the assumption of information efficiency, wherein the price of a traded asset reflects all available public information. However, organized manipulation deliberately distorts this price discovery mechanism.

\subsection{Taxonomy of Market Manipulation}
Modern financial regulation and academic literature categorize market manipulation into three primary classes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Action-Based Manipulation:} Actions executed by controlling corporate insiders, such as delaying material disclosures or artificial asset transfers.
    \item \textbf{Information-Based Manipulation:} Disseminating fraudulent or misleading rumors, exaggerated press releases, or coordinated social media hype (the classical "pump" communication channel).
    \item \textbf{Trade-Based Manipulation:} Artificial buy and sell trading patterns designed to create the illusion of active interest, such as wash trading, painting the tape, quote stuffing, and circular order routing.
\end{enumerate}

Pump-and-Dump schemes in emerging equity markets represent a toxic hybrid of information-based hype and trade-based liquidity manipulation. Manipulators exploit the structural vulnerability of thinly traded securities where small capital injections cause dramatic percentage price spikes.

\section{Review of Related Work}

\subsection{Natural Language Processing and Forum Analysis}
\noindent \textbf{Nam and Skillicorn (2021) --- Detecting Pump \& Dump from Online Forums} \\
Nam and Skillicorn examined the predictive power of social sentiment by analyzing message streams across unregulated investment forums \cite{nam2021detecting}. Their research demonstrated that organized pump-and-dump operations exhibit distinct linguistic patterns during the accumulation phase prior to price breakout. Using supervised natural language processing (NLP) classifiers, they achieved high classification accuracy in identifying targeted tokens. 

\textit{Analysis \& Critique:} While forum sentiment provides early pre-pump signals, relying strictly on text data introduces significant vulnerabilities. Manipulators frequently shift communication to encrypted, private channels (e.g., Telegram and WhatsApp groups) where public crawlers cannot reach. Furthermore, sentiment alone cannot measure the actual order-book capital absorption taking place on the exchange.

\subsection{Empirical Analysis of Pump Dynamics}
\noindent \textbf{Li, Shin, and Wang (2018) --- Empirical Analysis of Cryptocurrency Pump-and-Dumps} \\
Li et al. conducted an extensive empirical study across thousands of organized manipulation events in decentralized cryptocurrency markets \cite{li2018cryptocurrency}. The authors measured the speed of price escalation, peak volume ratios, and the duration of the subsequent dumping phase. Their findings revealed that modern algorithmic pumps reach their peak valuation within seconds to minutes, followed by an immediate liquidity dry-up where retail exit volume is trapped.

\textit{Analysis \& Critique:} The paper provides crucial empirical validation of volume and price trajectories during artificial pumps. However, the study is predominantly post-mortem, focusing on retrospective econometric profiling rather than building an active, real-time tripwire detection system.

\subsection{Network Topology and Order-Book Depletion}
\noindent \textbf{Massimo et al. (2020) --- Anatomy of Market Manipulation Strategies} \\
Massimo et al. analyzed the microstructure of financial manipulation using transaction graphs and order-book depth measurements \cite{massimo2020anatomy}. Their research established that manipulators systematically target assets with shallow order books where bid-ask spreads are wide. By placing aggressive market orders into thin liquidity, bad actors create steep price charts that attract algorithmic momentum scanners and retail chart followers.

\textit{Analysis \& Critique:} Massimo et al.'s framework highlights the vital role of order-book liquidity in distinguishing manipulation from genuine corporate buying. However, graph-theoretic models incur immense computational complexity, making them difficult to execute in sub-second retail browser terminals without heavy cloud cluster infrastructure.

\subsection{Options Derivatives and Open Interest Microstructure}
\noindent \textbf{Hull (2022) --- Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives} \\
John C. Hull's seminal work establishes the foundational mathematics of derivative pricing, the Greeks ($\Delta, \Gamma, \Theta, \nu$), and the structural behavior of option market makers \cite{hull2022options}. Option writers operate under asymmetric downside risk; when underlying spot prices breach key strike walls, option sellers are forced to buy back contracts or delta-hedge the underlying equity, triggering violent gamma squeeze feedback loops.
