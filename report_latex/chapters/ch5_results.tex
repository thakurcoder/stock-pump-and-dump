\section{Discussion of Results}

\subsection{Effectiveness of Volume Z-Scores vs. Raw Volume}
The empirical results confirm that raw volume alone is an unreliable indicator of market manipulation. In high-turnover benchmark equities (e.g., State Bank of India, \texttt{SBIN}), daily share volume regularly exceeds 20 million shares. However, because this volume lies within normal historical variance ($\sigma_V$), the standardized Z-score remains low ($Z_V = 1.1\sigma$). 

In contrast, in micro-cap equities, a sudden volume of 500,000 shares may represent an 800\% surge over the 20-day median ($Z_V = 4.8\sigma$). Standardizing against rolling variance allows SentinelMarket to effectively detect manipulation across the entire market spectrum.

\subsection{Latency and Resource Efficiency}
By utilizing local caching (\texttt{.cache/}) and in-memory vectorized calculations, the average response latency of the screening API is maintained under \textbf{1.2 seconds} for a 50-stock batch. This delivers a seamless, zero-lag experience for terminal users compared to cloud database queries that frequently suffer from network round-trip overhead.
